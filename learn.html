Capital structure explained

How a firm splits funding between debt and equity changes both its tax bill and its risk. These five ideas are the backbone of the simulator.

Modigliani–Miller

In 1958 Franco Modigliani and Merton Miller showed that, in a frictionless market with no taxes and no bankruptcy costs, firm value does not depend on the mix of debt and equity. Cheap debt is offset by a higher required return on the remaining equity. The pie is the same size; only the slices change.

VL = VU (no taxes, no distress)

The tax shield

Interest is tax-deductible in most corporate systems. Debt therefore reduces the government’s claim on operating profits. In a simple perpetuity, the present value of that shield is the tax rate times the amount of debt—so long as the firm can actually use the deduction.

VL = VU + Tc × D (MM with corporate taxes)

The gold band on the value chart is this extra slice sitting on top of unlevered value.

Financial distress costs

Leverage is not free. As coverage thins, lenders demand a higher spread, equity holders require a higher return, and the chance of costly distress (legal fees, fire sales, lost customers) rises. Those costs eat the tax advantage and can more than reverse it.

The simulator proxies this with a synthetic rating table: interest coverage maps to a spread, then a distress-sensitivity slider scales how aggressively that spread climbs with leverage.

Trade-off theory

Firms borrow until the marginal tax benefit equals the marginal cost of distress. That interior maximum is the “optimal” capital structure in this model. Stable cash-flow businesses (utilities) can support more debt; volatile or already-stressed businesses should carry less.

WACC

The weighted average cost of capital is the blended required return on the firm’s capital. After-tax debt is cheaper than equity, but adding debt also lifts both component costs. WACC therefore typically falls, then rises. The debt ratio that minimizes WACC is the same point that maximizes a zero-growth perpetuity of after-tax EBIT.

WACC = (E/V) Re + (D/V) Rd (1 − Tc)
Re = Rf + βL × ERP    βL = βu [1 + (1 − Tc) D/E]
